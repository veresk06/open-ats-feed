Nearly half of the ATS board links on the public web are dead ends.

We harvested every Greenhouse, Ashby, Lever, Breezy, Recruitee and Teamtailor board link in 17 Common Crawl indexes — 30,729 distinct company tokens — then called every one of them against the vendor's own public API. 16,361 answered with at least one posting open. 14,368 did not — 46.8% of the list: 10,416 are gone outright, and 3,952 still answer with nothing open. Everyone building a job feed harvests these tokens. This is what nobody publishes about them.

Live means the token resolved and the board had at least one posting open at the moment of reading. Empty means it resolved with zero postings — a real company with nothing open — and is counted as not live, which is the conservative choice. A correction to our own number

Erratum — Lever, 2026-09-03

We projected
1,721
We measured
1,621
Our stated 95% interval
1,591–1,852

Lever's API asks for one request per second, so a full pass takes about eighty minutes. Rather than wait, we probed a seeded random sample of 1,000 of the 4,961 tokens, got a 34.7% hit rate, scaled it, and published 1,721 live boards with a 95% confidence interval of 1,591–1,852.

The pass has since finished. The measured answer is 1,621 — 6.2% above the truth, and below the bottom of the interval we published with it. A 95% interval is allowed to miss one time in twenty; the honest reading is that our sample was not the uniform draw we treated it as, because liveness is not independent of where a token sits in the harvest.

We are leaving this here rather than quietly swapping the number, because a coverage figure you cannot audit is worth nothing, and the only way to show ours is auditable is to show it being corrected.

Sample the companies if you must. Never sample the postings.

Breezy was added to this index on the strength of a 250-token sample, then probed in full before anything was published. The sample got the board count nearly right — 3.9% out — and got the posting count wrong by 16.5%.

The two errors are not the same size because they are not the same kind of quantity. Whether a board is live is roughly a coin flip you can sample. How many postings it carries is long-tailed: the median Breezy board has four open roles and the largest carry hundreds, so a small draw almost always misses the boards that hold most of the corpus, and it misses them downward every time.

So the rule we now follow, and the reason every number on this page says how it was obtained: a company count may be projected from a sample and labelled as such. A posting count may not. It is measured or it is not published.

A 403 is a fact about your client, not a verdict about the company

Greenhouse starts returning 403 once a client has asked too often. Our verifier originally filed any non-429, non-5xx failure as dead — so throttling was being recorded as "this company has no board."

The tell was arithmetic, not a stack trace. A run over 10,091 Greenhouse tokens reported 4,391 live boards. An earlier run over 7,692 tokens — a strict subset of the same list — had reported 4,932. Adding tokens cannot remove companies, so one of the two runs was not measuring what it claimed. Splitting by status code found 2,107 403s in the new run against zero in the old, and 1,008 of those were boards the earlier run had confirmed live hours before.

A refused request is now retried, and one still refused after its retries is recorded as blocked — excluded from the denominator rather than counted as a miss. The general form of the mistake: when a service rate-limits you, the failure describes your client, and folding it into a verdict about the world biases the result downward while leaving it entirely believable.

Guessing a company's token across vendors does not work

A company's board token is often the same slug whichever ATS it runs — stripe, figma, ramp — so tokens harvested from one vendor look like free candidates for the others. Tested on seeded random samples of up to 400 tokens per direction, it is worth 2–3% marginal coverage, and 0.3–0.5% into Lever. Hypothesis rejected. Published so nobody has to rediscover it.

What this measurement does not tell you

A board is findable here only if something on the public web linked to it while Common Crawl was looking. Companies that never linked their board anywhere crawlable are missing, and there is no way to count what you cannot see. This is a floor, not a census.

It also says nothing about freshness. The dead tokens measure the decay rate of an archive-aged list; they do not say how fast new companies appear, which is the number a re-sweep cadence should be tuned to. And the 3,952 boards that answered with nothing open are not classified — seasonally quiet and permanently dormant look identical from outside.

There is no Workday here, and no Taleo. Both are large, and both need a different method than a public unauthenticated board API.
